# Mez Systems product-expression ethos

Status: approved Phase A guidance

Authority: `DEC-PRODUCT-CARD-VISUAL-ARCHITECTURE-001`

Evidence: Product Card 02 rounds 01–10, culminating in 32 keep verdicts at `H-EXP-04A-CARD-VISUAL-DIRECTION` on 22 July 2026

This document captures the strongest available evidence of Olli's visual taste for Mez Systems. It is not a moodboard and it is not permission to copy the Round 10 specimens into every context. It is a set of compositional instincts, failure boundaries and review habits that future foundations, expressions, components and channels must inherit.

## The design character

Mez Systems should feel precise, calm, intelligent and materially alive. Premium comes from hierarchy, proportion, restraint and exact repetition—not luxury styling, decorative noise or fashionable effects. The product material is the visual protagonist. Typography, Wings, surfaces and controls give it a disciplined system around which to perform.

The family should feel recognisably Mez before colour is applied. Rounded geometry, generous spacing, quiet monochrome surfaces, exact Wings and a product-first information hierarchy do that work. Gradient material adds product identity and energy; it does not compensate for weak layout.

## Approved invariants

1. **Product name first.** Lead with the canonical public product name, then its extended system name, then the supporting job sentence. Internal codes, gradient IDs, slashes, numbered editions and invented classification labels are not customer-facing hierarchy.
2. **Rounded is the brand default.** Outer card, plate, field, control and section geometry follows the approved rounded scale. An internal material/footer join may become flat where the two surfaces meet, while the outside silhouette stays rounded.
3. **Filled actions are the product-led default.** Landing plates, reusable product cards and product heroes use a consistent filled primary action. Outline and text actions remain secondary roles, not arbitrary visual variants.
4. **Wings are composed, not sprinkled.** Centred or mark-above Wings may be larger when identity is the focal event. Corner Wings are smaller supporting identity. Use the exact canonical asset; do not force a disc, sphere, monogram or Wings placement merely because it exists in the kit.
5. **One material, many bounded expressions.** Plate, full field, portrait, stack, fan, box, capsule, disc and sphere are selectable postures with jobs. They are not a checklist that every component must contain.
6. **Static and animated are twins.** Show both where motion matters. A focal Living Core is alive by default; no animation button is required. Only one Deep Mineral No. 5 core may run in a viewport. Repeated products, bundle contents and supporting objects remain static. Reduced motion and renderer failure use the exact static twin without layout shift.
7. **Light and dark are complementary.** Use canonical F8, white and charcoal surfaces. Focal contrast may place a dark object in a light room or a light object in a dark room. Contained dark uses the approved near-charcoal surfaces, never pure black. Product material carries the colour.
8. **Show components doing real work.** Approve cards inside believable heroes, shelves, explainers, product pages, bundles and commerce journeys. An isolated art card cannot prove website hierarchy, responsive behavior or component usefulness.
9. **Families compare; bundles relate.** Product shelves preserve equal sibling geometry. Bundles make the relationship and package legible through stacks, measured fans, contained boxes or an integrated offer—not arbitrary overlapping decoration.
10. **Future products inherit safely.** Components read canonical registry data, avoid fixed counts and use the declared default proof territory until a new product receives its own assignment.

## What Olli consistently rejected

- Generic SaaS, bento and “vibe-coded” card styling with no authored product idea.
- A disc, sphere, Wings mark, pill or gradient object forced into a composition without a clear job.
- Sharp outer corners that contradict the approved geometry ethos.
- Decorative IDs, numbers, slashes, archive codes, fake edition language and unexplained labels.
- Pills with no useful content or interaction role.
- Extended system names leading the public product name.
- Inconsistent outline, text and filled actions across equivalent product cards.
- Pure black, white outline strokes on dark objects, ambient card shadows, glow and glass used as premium shortcuts.
- Manual “animate” controls, multiple moving cores and motion applied to Wings, type or layout.
- Collector or scarcity framing inside ordinary product discovery. System Editions were removed from Product Card 02; a future trading-card programme needs its own gate.
- Broad aesthetic resets after a clear human signal appears.
- Large specimen counts that change only decoration instead of composition, hierarchy, proportion or context.

## The ten-round learning trail

| Round | What it taught the system |
|---|---|
| 01 | Ten isolated art-card treatments did not answer whether these were real website components. |
| 02 | Generic, over-decorated cards can be polished and still feel unowned. |
| 03 | Forced discs, spheres and editorial devices read as vibe-coded when the layout has no product job. |
| 04 | Expression breadth must include different compositions, proportions and real page contexts—not more gradients alone. |
| 05 | Fifty specimens improved visibility but breadth without a coherent design world remained below the required standard. |
| 06 | Invented aesthetic “worlds” broke canonical rounded geometry and hid the established Mez vocabulary; manual animation was rejected. |
| 07 | Returning to canonical materials, rounded anatomy and realistic sections found the first strong language. Granular keep/revise/kill feedback became essential. |
| 08 | Controlled variants exposed the exact preferences: centred or larger Wings, flat internal joins, integrated footers, cleaner bundles and fewer decorative systems. |
| 09 | Convergence produced the final rules: remove editions, lead with public names, use filled actions, enlarge requested Wings and repair fan spacing. |
| 10 | All 32 surviving specimens were kept. The visual grammar was approved and Phase A closed. |

## How agents should work from this evidence

Start with the approved invariants and the job of the component. Produce meaningfully different compositions—not surface reskins—and show them in realistic contexts at desktop and mobile sizes. Use canonical product data and existing expression primitives, but choose only the primitive that improves the job.

Keep review atomic. Every specimen and every family must be independently addressable with keep, revise, kill and written feedback. Once a direction receives a clear keep signal, preserve it as a champion and vary one or two controlled dimensions around it. Do not reset the whole visual language unless the human explicitly asks.

Preserve the full decision trail. Rejected work is negative design data, not clutter to erase. Future agents should read the synthesis first and pull raw round receipts only when they need exact evidence or are about to revisit a rejected boundary.

## Phase B inheritance

Phase B is the Website Component System. It begins from this locked visual grammar and adds function; it does not run a second visual-direction competition.

Round 02 clarified an important architectural rule: **Phase A must not be rendered as a separate gallery inside Phase B.** Its 32 keeps are the visual construction language beneath the functional components. A product hero, pricing card, checkout summary or bundle should visibly inherit a Phase A silhouette, hierarchy and material rule while doing its own job. Repeating the approved specimens beside the functional work makes the system feel fractured rather than inherited.

The Round 02 functional feedback adds these working principles:

1. **A single-product hero is an identity event.** Use a large centred Wings mark over one automatically live focal material when that treatment is selected. Preserve product-name-first hierarchy and make the filled actions feel substantial; small button type undermines the premium scale.
2. **Family discovery needs hierarchy, not maximum simultaneous density.** Five products may be present without five equally loud tall cards. Use a lead product, calm sibling objects, measured rails or staged disclosure while keeping essential family access visible.
3. **Marketing comparisons should read as authored sections, not application tables.** Starting-point matrices may retain semantic relationships but need editorial pacing, generous type and product material.
4. **Bento cells are full compositions.** Use full-bleed material, graphics, media, proof or overlays that fill their cells. Avoid small inset cards inside larger cards. At most one cell may carry the automatic Living Core.
5. **Integration identity must be real and quiet.** Use the actual integration marks or clear wordmarks with restrained separation; do not create a soup of filled logo boxes.
6. **Do not double-mark full overlays.** When product material already fills the full enterprise or campaign field, omit Wings unless the mark has a necessary identity job.
7. **Bundle motion may communicate relationship.** A focused stack may cycle emphasis between static cards or allocate one live material, provided Wings, type and layout remain still, reduced motion preserves the composition and no second WebGL core runs.
8. **Compact rails are contextual navigation.** Keep them only when they answer a real “next system” or related-product job. They are not a primary discovery substitute.
