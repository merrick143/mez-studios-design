## Mechanisms inferred from the evidence

These are interpretations, not statements of Ramp's private intent.

1. **Operations become the identity:** counters, transaction objects, receipts, invoice states, data charts, and savings outcomes repeat more often than decorative brand art. `RAMP-E-001`, `RAMP-E-002`, `RAMP-E-004`, `RAMP-E-008`.
2. **One platform is stronger than separate product brands:** the portfolio uses a stable parent and neutral shared cards, then differentiates with operational objects and use-case copy. `RAMP-E-004`, `RAMP-E-005`, `RAMP-E-006`.
3. **Proof has several levels:** live-looking platform activity, mechanism snapshots, customer outcomes, company datasets, reports, and attributed economic analysis each support a different claim type. `RAMP-E-002`, `RAMP-E-003`, `RAMP-E-008`, `RAMP-E-009`, `RAMP-E-010`.
4. **Editorial authority has its own density mode:** the Economics Lab reduces product theatrics, introduces named experts and methodology, and lets data cards carry the visual interest. `RAMP-E-007`, `RAMP-E-008`, `RAMP-E-009`.
5. **Operational taxonomy supports transfer:** recurring product-release categories, help-centre object definitions, report series, and data-use rules expose stable concepts outside the interface. `RAMP-E-010`, `RAMP-E-011`, `RAMP-E-012`, `RAMP-E-013`.

## Category conventions versus distinctive mechanisms

| Category convention | More distinctive in this sample |
| --- | --- |
| Black wordmark, large sans-serif hero, bright CTA, product card, customer logos | Small operational counters and a persistent agent-activity strip embedded into the marketing narrative |
| Multi-product suite presented as a card grid | Each major homepage tile uses a different operational object, notification, document, data table, account view, or integration matrix as proof |
| Customer testimonial and headline statistic | A large customer result inside a specific story tile, followed by a direct report path |
| Branded content hub and downloadable reports | A named Economics Lab with visible researchers, interactive datasets, update cadence, chart cards, and methodology paths |
| Release notes and help centre | Recurring public taxonomies that connect products, operational objects, releases, reports, and data distribution rules |

## Transfer principles for Mez

These principles describe what can be learned without copying Ramp's expression.

- `RQ-01`: Let real system activity and evidence markers become recurring recognition devices. They must be truthful, interpretable, and usable without the logo or gradient.
- `RQ-02`: A shared parent chassis can hold multiple products if each product owns a different operational object, proof sequence, density, and result type. A uniform product grid alone is insufficient differentiation.
- `RQ-03`: Build an evidence ladder. Use activity evidence for current state, interface evidence for mechanism, customer evidence for outcome, and research evidence for broader claims.
- `RQ-04`: Give each channel a job-specific density. Hero marketing establishes the promise, the product matrix demonstrates mechanisms, the research hub compresses metrics, and long-form analysis exposes method and uncertainty.
- `RQ-05`: Keep parent attribution compact and stable on mobile, but do not allow the product layer to disappear into generic cards. Define a product-level cue that remains legible without creating four sub-brands.
- `RQ-07`: Publish stable operational nouns, release categories, evidence definitions, report series, and representation constraints so humans and LLMs can reuse the system safely.

## Source signatures to exclude

The following are Ramp-associated expressions and must not be reproduced in Mez work:

- Ramp's wordmark, rising mark, card design, custom typeface, icons, interface crops, customer evidence, charts, and copy.
- Ramp's particular black, white, pale neutral, and fluorescent yellow-green combination.
- The dotted numeric field, agent-activity ticker, casual-or-serious control, wave-line card treatment, and exact operational counter categories.
- Ramp's product names, six-card product grid, Economics Lab name and composition, research-card layout, report taxonomy, and release filters.
- Any customer saving, company statistic, adoption figure, or platform activity value without independent Mez evidence and provenance.

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Ramp's authenticated product, advertisements, emails, decks, report files, customer case details, internal design library, and live data pipeline were not inspected.
- The reproducible package includes the standard `1440 px`, `1280 px`, and `390 px` widths. Visual conclusions for Economics Lab, Reports, Releases, Help, and the Data Partner Program still rely on the separate manual observation because automatic representative selection chose other public Ramp surfaces.
- The homepage counters appeared operational, but refresh cadence, sampling, latency, and whether all figures were live were not verified.
- The product matrix is persuasive public storytelling, not direct proof that each workflow behaves exactly as depicted.
- The products page creates breadth and clarity but gives its six products limited visual individuality in the visible desktop and mobile overview. Differentiation relies heavily on names, job statements, and later mechanism content.
- Public evidence for design-to-code or Figma transfer is materially weaker than Stripe's. Ramp demonstrates semantic transfer through releases, help, reports, and data rules, not a public multi-format design-system contract.
- Immediate recognition still depends substantially on Ramp's wordmark, rising mark, fluorescent action colour, card, and dotted field. The neutral card chassis alone is not distinctive enough to transfer as a recognition solution.
- First-party customer and research claims were treated as attributed evidence. They were not independently audited or validated for Mez use.
