Decision 02 · Hierarchy

One action leads. The others get out of its way.

Every group gets one primary action. Secondary actions keep a visible boundary. Tertiary actions stay typographic. Equal visual weight is not flexibility, it is indecision.

Decision 04 · Scale

Three sizes. Three jobs.

Compact controls belong in navigation and dense utilities. Default controls handle most decisions. Prominent controls appear only where conversion or completion is the primary job.

Decision 05 · Icons

Icons clarify movement. They do not decorate labels.

Directional actions may carry one trailing arrow. Familiar utilities may use a standalone icon with an accessible name. Purchase, save and destructive labels stay literal.

Decision 06 · Dark surface

Invert the hierarchy, not the whole language.

Dark is a section treatment, never a theme. The primary action becomes white. Secondary stays transparent with a visible light boundary. Product gradients never enter the control.

Decision 07 · Mobile

One thumb. One obvious next step.

On small screens the primary fills the available width. The secondary becomes a quiet text action beneath it. Paired full-width bricks are reserved for two equally consequential choices.
