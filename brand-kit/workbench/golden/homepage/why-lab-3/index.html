The allocation rule, from the study of the references you sent: one material cell per bento. It carries the proof or the product, and every other cell is paper or charcoal. Colour is only expensive against restraint. Round one's variants 4 and 7 put material on every cell and that is exactly what made them read cheap.

The grid is now an object. Every bento sits in a frame: 32px outer radius, 8px padding, 24px cells, so the radii are concentric by construction rather than by eye. Three on-token neutrals stack, neutral-25 page, neutral-100 frame, neutral-0 cells, which is what gives the frame its edge without adding a border.

Two changes to the head, both treatment and not copy: the eyebrow is a hairline pill so the head has a defined start, and the title is two-tone, grey lead-in and ink payload, so one sentence carries its own emphasis without a second type size.

Variant 4 keeps registry material without spending its colour event: the shipped product is a material cell, the four coming ones are paper cells carrying a small material disc as an identity token. Variant 7 is new, replacing round one's all-material quilt with the light-and-dark pair that appears in four of the eight references. Copy is byte-identical across all ten.
