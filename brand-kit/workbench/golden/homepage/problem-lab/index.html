V1 Editorial Thesis Treating the problem as a magazine feature — a de-emphasised setup line collapsing into an oversized "Too many tools. Too many middlemen." subject, a single masthead rule, an anaphoric "Another…" symptom stack, and an Instrument-Serif turn line — lets proportion, weight and whitespace carry the argument, so the fragmentation reads as a considered thesis rather than a decorated feature grid.

The problem

AI was supposed to make the business simpler. Too many tools.
Too many middlemen.

The models keep improving. The tools keep changing. And the business keeps explaining itself from the beginning — every single time.

What actually got added

  • Another interface.
  • Another subscription.
  • Another credit system.
  • Another isolated window that knows nothing about the rest of the company.

What it actually costs

The same intelligence, repackaged. You get more limits and less control — the intermediary picks the models, the features, the credits, the rate limits and the upgrade schedule. And your context — every prompt, the workflow logic, the accumulated business memory — stays trapped inside a tool you don't control.

External tools can create real value. Dependency without ownership cannot.

Your business needs an operating layer it controls.

The messy pile then resolves into a single calm owned layer, so the composition itself argues for ownership.
