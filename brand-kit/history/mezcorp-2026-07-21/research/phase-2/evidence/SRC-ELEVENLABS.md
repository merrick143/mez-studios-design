## Observed evidence

### Identity and recognition

#### `ELV-E01` The parent symbol compresses name and product behaviour

- Surface: brand guidelines.
- Observation: the parent shorthand is constructed from two simple vertical lines. ElevenLabs explains that it abstracts the number in the company name and also references a pause control associated with audio.
- Mechanism: one low-complexity sign carries both linguistic recognition and a category-relevant action. It remains suitable for constrained avatars and app contexts.
- Questions: `RQ-01`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E02` Recognition at small sizes is designed as a holding-device system

- Surface: brand guidelines.
- Observation: the symbol is centred with proportional surrounding space and is explicitly shown inside circular, rounded-square, and square containers for avatars and apps.
- Mechanism: the invariant is the symbol, centring, and clearance relationship, while the outer device adapts to channel requirements.
- Questions: `RQ-01`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E03` Audio behaviour extends identity beyond static graphics

- Surface: parent brand guidance, Text to Speech, ElevenCreative, and product articles.
- Observation: the symbol references pause; public pages repeatedly expose play, voice, timeline, language, pacing, emotion, and duration controls. Some API graphics use kinetic typography and ElevenMusic materials show transformed playback symbols.
- Mechanism: interaction objects native to the medium can reinforce identity across icon, demonstration, motion, and product behaviour.
- Questions: `RQ-01`, `RQ-06`.
- Confidence: high for the repeated semantic connection, medium for cross-page motion behaviour because animation timing was not measured.

### Product family and architecture

#### `ELV-E04` The family has a stable parent and deliberately distinct platform territories

- Surface: brand guidelines.
- Observation: ElevenAgents, ElevenCreative, and ElevenAPI each have a dedicated logotype, naming rule, palette, and graphic approach. Agents uses a blue-led system with circular or spherical representations and tactile imagery. Creative uses an orange-led system and Chladni-pattern graphics. API uses monochrome and combines related patterns with kinetic typography.
- Mechanism: the family shares parent naming discipline and audio-derived conceptual material while each platform owns a recognisable colour, motif, and audience posture.
- Questions: `RQ-02`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E05` Platform distinction follows audience and mode of work

- Surface: docs overview and platform pages.
- Observation: ElevenCreative is positioned as a no-code production workspace, ElevenAgents as a builder and operating platform for conversations, ElevenAPI as programmatic infrastructure, and Reception AI as a packaged business solution. The differences are functional and audience-based, not only visual.
- Mechanism: portfolio architecture begins with user, job, interface mode, and delivery model; visual territories then make those differences easier to recognise.
- Questions: `RQ-02`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E06` Shared concepts create family coherence below platform branding

- Surface: docs overview.
- Observation: voices, models, credits, languages, and dedicated capabilities recur across platform paths. A capability can appear in a no-code workspace, through an API, or inside an agent experience without changing its core conceptual identity.
- Mechanism: common objects and infrastructure provide the chassis, while each platform changes the workflow and proof.
- Questions: `RQ-02`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

### Claim to literal proof

#### `ELV-E07` The capability is often available at the point of the claim

- Surface: Text to Speech and ElevenCreative.
- Observation: claims about expressive speech are paired with sample text, voice identity, model choice, and controls for qualities such as speed, tone, pacing, and style. Creative presents a prompt and generated-media workflow rather than only a decorative screenshot.
- Mechanism: the proof object is interactive or playback-ready, so the audience can inspect the medium the product claims to improve.
- Questions: `RQ-03`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E08` Dubbing proof decomposes quality into observable mechanisms

- Surface: Dubbing v2.
- Observation: the page connects localisation claims to source-audio conditioning, performance preservation, voice cloning, language choice, synchronisation, upload or URL input, and example work items with language and duration metadata.
- Mechanism: a large qualitative promise is decomposed into inputs, transformations, preserved properties, controls, and outputs.
- Questions: `RQ-03`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E09` Agent claims are paired with channel, latency, workflow, and control evidence

- Surface: ElevenAgents.
- Observation: the public page connects natural conversation claims to real-time turn detection, stated response latency, configurable voice, warm transfer, telephony and widget deployment, API access, simulation, analytics, guardrails, integrations, and example test results.
- Mechanism: emotional benefit and operational proof are shown together. The proof includes both capability and governance.
- Questions: `RQ-03`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E10` A release article shows human control around agent action

- Surface: Studio Agent release.
- Observation: the co-editor drafts onto a media timeline, asks clarifying questions, can work in planning or editing modes, accepts human interruption, preserves manual changes, and resumes from the edited state. It also maps requested audio events to frame-level positions.
- Mechanism: AI proof is credible when permissions, artefact changes, time position, human override, and continuation behaviour are explicit.
- Questions: `RQ-03`, `RQ-06`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

### Density across channels

#### `ELV-E11` Marketing density follows the sensory complexity of the product

- Surface: ElevenCreative, Text to Speech, Dubbing v2, and ElevenAgents.
- Observation: pages alternate concise narrative with media players, voice selectors, prompt inputs, workflow screenshots, language controls, performance claims, integrations, testimonials, and governance evidence. Density rises when the user needs to inspect a capability and relaxes around positioning or calls to action.
- Mechanism: density is allocated to moments of evaluation. A media product needs enough controls and output context to make quality assessable.
- Questions: `RQ-04`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E12` Documentation compresses the family into paths and shared concepts

- Surface: docs overview.
- Observation: documentation explains four ways to use the company technology, then defines common concepts before offering separate paths and model-level detail.
- Mechanism: progressive disclosure starts with portfolio orientation, moves to a shared conceptual model, then branches by task and implementation depth.
- Questions: `RQ-02`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

### Motion and state

#### `ELV-E13` Motion semantics come from sound and time

- Surface: brand guidance, Text to Speech, Dubbing v2, and Studio Agent release.
- Observation: pause and play, speaking and waiting, waveform or pattern response, timeline position, synchronisation, duration, generation, and handoff are repeatedly meaningful states.
- Mechanism: motion can express temporal structure and media transformation rather than serve as an ambient AI effect.
- Questions: `RQ-06`.
- Confidence: high for the semantic vocabulary, medium for actual animation behaviour.

#### `ELV-E14` Agent motion is conversational turn-taking and controlled handoff

- Surface: ElevenAgents and Studio Agent release.
- Observation: turn detection determines when to speak, pause, or wait. Studio Agent can move between plan and create permissions, yield to a human, and resume after manual edits.
- Mechanism: orchestration is communicated through turn ownership, permission state, interruption, continuation, and synchronisation.
- Questions: `RQ-06`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

### System transfer and governance

#### `ELV-E15` Naming rules and visual territories are explicit at platform level

- Surface: brand guidelines.
- Observation: each platform has prescribed written form, logotype, palette role, and graphic mechanism, along with named incorrect forms. The parent mark has separate clearance and small-space guidance.
- Mechanism: a portfolio transfers reliably when identity rules are scoped to hierarchy levels and include both positive and negative instructions.
- Questions: `RQ-02`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E16` Capabilities transfer through stable identifiers and multiple delivery surfaces

- Surface: docs overview and ElevenCreative.
- Observation: voices have identifiers, models have explicit performance characteristics, credits form a shared consumption model, and core capabilities are available in the web application and through APIs or SDKs.
- Mechanism: shared IDs, model names, concepts, and delivery contracts let the same capability move between no-code production, application UI, and code.
- Questions: `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

#### `ELV-E17` Governance is included in the product proof

- Surface: ElevenCreative and ElevenAgents.
- Observation: public material includes workspaces, shared resources, roles, approvals, audit logs, model approvals, testing, analytics, and guardrails alongside creative and conversational capability.
- Mechanism: enterprise transfer is not complete until permissions, review, evaluation, and compliance are part of the system model.
- Questions: `RQ-03`, `RQ-07`.
- Confidence: high.

## Category conventions versus distinctive mechanisms

### Category conventions

- Product-led marketing with interactive demos or media samples.
- Large performance claims supported by customer logos and testimonials.
- Platform navigation split by creator, developer, and enterprise audiences.
- Bright colour territories for AI products.
- Gradient imagery, abstract forms, generated media, and expressive motion.
- API documentation, SDK examples, and integration galleries.

These are category patterns. They should not shape a Mez direction by themselves.

### More distinctive ElevenLabs mechanisms

- A minimal parent sign that is simultaneously a name abstraction and an audio control reference.
- Platform identities that have explicit differences in palette and graphic approach while sharing parent naming and audio concepts.
- Chladni-pattern and kinetic-type territories rooted in sound behaviour rather than generic computation imagery.
- Proof that lets users hear, configure, compare, or inspect the exact medium being claimed.
- Agent demonstrations that expose turn-taking, latency, deployment channel, testing, guardrails, and human handoff.
- The same capability transferred through web workflow, stable conceptual IDs, APIs, and SDKs.

## Transfer principles for Mez

These principles are abstracted. They do not authorise use of ElevenLabs styling.

1. Ground a recognition mechanism in what the Mez system does, such as routing, transforming, validating, connecting, or resolving, rather than attaching an arbitrary futuristic motif.
2. Define family invariants at the parent level, then give each product a different audience posture, proof object, state vocabulary, data shape, and campaign territory.
3. Make colour only one layer of product distinction. The product should remain identifiable in monochrome through motif behaviour and proof form.
4. Decompose claims into source input, system action, preserved or changed properties, visible output, and verification.
5. Put inspectable proof at the point of the claim wherever the channel supports it. In static channels, preserve the same evidence chain as a sequence of frames.
6. Author motion around time, ownership, permission, handoff, synchronisation, and completion.
7. Use stable IDs and concepts so an approved capability can be represented consistently in Figma, production code, content templates, and LLM context.
8. Treat approvals, auditability, evaluation, and safety states as first-class design-system material, not hidden enterprise copy.

## Limitations and uncertainty

- No authenticated ElevenLabs product was inspected. Workspace and application behaviour is supported only by public demonstrations, documentation, and official descriptions.
- Audio was not copied, measured, or independently evaluated, so quality claims are recorded as source claims rather than verified performance conclusions.
- Dynamic animation timing, easing, responsive state, hover behaviour, and reduced-motion treatment were not measured.
- Public marketing contains curated examples and stated metrics that can change. Counts and latency figures are evidence of proof structure, not proposed Mez claims.
- The brand page explicitly documents three principal platforms and also includes ElevenMusic, while the documentation presents four usage paths including Reception AI. This indicates an evolving portfolio, so current hierarchy should not be assumed permanent.
- Exact visual values and source assets were deliberately not recorded because they are not transferable Mez inputs.

## Study conclusion

ElevenLabs' most useful lesson is its separation of parent coherence from platform distinction. The family is held together by naming, shared audio concepts, capability objects, and delivery infrastructure, while platforms differ through audience, job, proof, colour role, and graphic behaviour. Its second useful lesson is literal proof: sound, timing, transformation, controls, deployment, and governance appear close to the claims. For Mez, the opportunity is to design each product from its own job and evidence model while sharing a rigorous system chassis. The risk is copying highly recognisable source signatures such as audio-derived patterns, spheres, playback symbols, bright platform palettes, or sensory AI spectacle.
