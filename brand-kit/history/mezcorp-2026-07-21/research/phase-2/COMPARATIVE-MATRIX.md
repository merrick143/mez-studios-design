## `M-01` Distributed recognition

**Research questions:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-08`  
**Problem:** Mez currently asks wings, gradients, discs, and repeated card arrangements to carry too much identity. Removing those devices risks leaving a generic software composition.

### Source observations

- **Notion:** recognition persists through editorial scale, neutral space, literal product framing, small expressive punctuation, and relational spacing rules. Evidence: `NTN-E01`, `NTN-E02`, `NTN-E03`.
- **Linear:** monochrome discipline, generous clearance, numbered operating language, and restrained hierarchy recur without constant reliance on brand colour. Evidence: `LIN-E01`, `LIN-E02`, `LIN-E03`.
- **Stripe:** wordmark, ruled chassis, diagonal transitions, typographic scale, and interface or document proof reinforce one another. Evidence: `STRIPE-E-001`, `STRIPE-E-002`, `STRIPE-E-011`.
- **ElevenLabs:** a simple parent sign, adaptive holding devices, and product-native audio states connect identity across static and interactive contexts. Evidence: `ELV-E01`, `ELV-E02`, `ELV-E03`.
- **Ramp:** operational counters, activity evidence, transaction objects, and data cards repeat alongside the parent identity. Evidence: `RAMP-E-001`, `RAMP-E-002`, `RAMP-E-008`.

### Cross-source mechanism

Recognition is distributed across several independent behaviours: composition, type hierarchy, information geometry, proof framing, state language, density transition, motion purpose, and voice. A robust identity does not collapse when any one device is removed.

### Transferable principle

Define and test at least four Mez-specific, non-colour recognition behaviours. Each behaviour must derive from Mez's operating model, remain usable in monochrome, and work across marketing and operational surfaces. Marks and gradients may reinforce the stack but cannot count as the stack.

### Exact expressions to avoid

- Notion's outlined character cast, circular face row, inline software capsule, block metaphor, and exact editorial hero composition.
- Linear's numbered figure notation, sparse dark software shell, desaturated blue identity, and exact workflow-stage sequence.
- Stripe's iridescent ribbon, fine-rule proportions, diagonal cuts, and violet, cyan, pink, orange, and navy combination.
- ElevenLabs' two-bar pause sign, Chladni graphics, voice orbs, playback-symbol transformations, and kinetic audio typography.
- Ramp's fluorescent yellow-green action, dotted field, agent ticker, operational counter categories, and physical card treatment.

### Scope and channels

Homepage and landing pages, product UI, paid and organic social, email, reports, presentations, documentation covers, product launch assets, static and motion identity moments.

### Confidence and epistemic status

Confidence is **high**. All five sources show recognition as a repeated combination rather than one device. Epistemic status is **strong inference** because causality has not been established through blind recognition testing.

### Originality risk

Risk is **high**. Each source's combination is recognisable and easy to imitate superficially. Mitigation requires monochrome Mez-native prototypes, a multi-source derivation record, and blind review asking whether the result resembles a named source.

### Tension or contradiction

- Quiet structural repetition can become ownable, but neutral typography and cards alone become generic.
- A simple device can improve small-size recognition, but ElevenLabs also shows how quickly one behaviour-linked sign becomes source property.
- Ramp demonstrates operational proof as identity, yet its most immediate recognition still depends heavily on mark, action colour, and card.

### Candidate experiments

- `E-01` Monochrome recognition test.
- `E-03` Claim-proof transformation, used to test whether the recognition stack survives channel compression.

**Decision status:** `UNAPPROVED`.

## `M-02` Shared semantic chassis, distinct product territory

**Research questions:** `RQ-02`, `RQ-05`, `RQ-08`  
**Problem:** AI OS, Aurora, Prism, and Forge risk becoming one template with different names, gradients, and screenshots, or four unrelated mini-brands that lose Mez Systems attribution.

### Source observations

- **Notion:** products share typography, navigation, controls, frames, and content rhythm while owning different verbs, information objects, proof states, and operating models. Evidence: `NTN-E04`, `NTN-E05`, `NTN-E06`, `NTN-E07`, `NTN-E08`.
- **Linear:** one workflow chassis spans intake, planning, building, review, and monitoring, while stable objects recur across marketing, product documentation, and integrations. Evidence: `LIN-E04`, `LIN-E05`.
- **Stripe:** a stable parent layer and local product layer survive narrow widths; constrained extension rules allow bounded app-level identity without breaking platform consistency. Evidence: `STRIPE-E-003`, `STRIPE-E-005`, `STRIPE-E-006`.
- **ElevenLabs:** parent naming and shared capability concepts hold the family together, while each platform owns a user, job, workflow, proof form, delivery model, and explicit visual territory. Evidence: `ELV-E04`, `ELV-E05`, `ELV-E06`.
- **Ramp:** one parent and neutral chassis span the portfolio, while operational objects differentiate homepage stories. Its product overview also demonstrates the failure mode of limited individuality in a uniform grid. Evidence: `RAMP-E-004`, `RAMP-E-005`, `RAMP-E-006`.

### Cross-source mechanism

Family coherence comes from stable semantic primitives, naming, controls, accessibility, parent attribution, and state logic. Product distinction comes from the primary user, job, dominant information object, proof primitive, result type, density, state transition, motion role, and campaign posture.

### Transferable principle

Give every Mez product a territory contract across shared and product-owned axes. Approval must occur in monochrome and without product names before accent colour or campaign art is considered.

### Exact expressions to avoid

- Notion's product icons, block-based workspace model, Calendar stacked-date mark, Mail interface language, and exact suite hierarchy.
- Linear's intake-to-monitor workflow, issue and project object names, numbered chapters, sidebar, diff, and status treatments.
- Stripe's two-level navigation arrangement, app-indicator boundary, payment product taxonomy, and extension-component styling.
- ElevenLabs' fused platform naming, blue, orange, and monochrome allocation, audio-derived motifs, and creator-agent-API structure.
- Ramp's six-card product grid, finance product names, parent-only mobile list, and differentiation based only on job copy.

### Scope and channels

Master-brand and product navigation, small endorsements, app icons, product landing pages, product UI shells, portfolio overviews, launch campaigns, social tiles, email, reports, documentation, templates.

### Confidence and epistemic status

Confidence is **high** for semantic family architecture and **medium** for small-size Mez hierarchy because only Stripe, ElevenLabs, Linear, and Ramp provide direct compact-hierarchy evidence. Epistemic status is **strong inference**.

### Originality risk

Risk is **high**. Product territories can become source-inspired skins or overbuilt sub-brands. Mitigation requires an explicit no-source-signature checklist, parent-level invariants, and distinction tests without colour or names.

### Tension or contradiction

- Shared primitives improve transfer, but excessive uniformity creates Ramp's neutral-grid failure mode.
- Strong local identities improve product recognition, but ElevenLabs shows the governance burden and imitation risk of platform-specific graphic territories.
- Compact parent attribution protects family trust, but too little product cue makes small outputs interchangeable.

### Candidate experiments

- `E-02` Four-product territory matrix.
- `E-05` Semantic object portability pilot, including parent and product mapping.

**Decision status:** `UNAPPROVED`.

## `M-03` Claim-to-proof adjacency

**Research questions:** `RQ-03`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-08`  
**Problem:** Large Mez claims can drift into decorative mockups, uncited metrics, or vague AI language that does not reveal the mechanism, evidence class, or human control.

### Source observations

- **Notion:** claims are followed by populated product objects; trust claims resolve into permissions, logs, reversibility, and controls. Evidence: `NTN-E09`, `NTN-E10`, `NTN-E11`, `NTN-E12`, `NTN-E13`.
- **Linear:** realistic work states connect claims to objects, state transitions, changed artefacts, provenance, and human review. Changelog publishing makes the proof grammar routine. Evidence: `LIN-E06`, `LIN-E07`, `LIN-E08`.
- **Stripe:** a checkout claim, attributed metric, and literal payment states share one reading sequence. Reporting and company proof use distinct output and attribution models. Evidence: `STRIPE-E-004`, `STRIPE-E-010`, `STRIPE-E-012`.
- **ElevenLabs:** product claims expose input, controls, transformation, preserved properties, output, deployment conditions, guardrails, and handoff. Evidence: `ELV-E07`, `ELV-E08`, `ELV-E09`, `ELV-E10`.
- **Ramp:** platform activity, mechanism snapshots, customer outcomes, research cards, dated analysis, and data rules form distinct proof classes. Evidence: `RAMP-E-002`, `RAMP-E-003`, `RAMP-E-004`, `RAMP-E-008`, `RAMP-E-009`, `RAMP-E-013`.

### Cross-source mechanism

Credible proof keeps the claim close to its input, mechanism, state change, output, result, verification, control, exception, or decision. Evidence classes remain distinct so interface demonstration, customer outcome, company metric, and aggregate research cannot impersonate one another.

### Transferable principle

Create one structured Mez proof record with claim, evidence class, input, action, visible state, output, result, source, method or verification, exception, and human decision. Each channel may reveal a bounded subset but cannot invent or change the underlying proof.

### Exact expressions to avoid

- Notion's sample workspaces, database objects, agent examples, and software-object-in-headline treatment.
- Linear's issue titles, repository contexts, diff states, agent-session layout, and exact claim-to-workflow composition.
- Stripe's checkout UI, payment success states, transaction data, revenue metrics, and customer claims.
- ElevenLabs' prompts, voices, players, waveform or timeline controls, latency claims, and media examples.
- Ramp's live-looking ticker, finance notifications, invoice and savings examples, customer outcomes, charts, and research-card layout.

### Scope and channels

Marketing pages, product UI, demonstrations, advertising, email, sales materials, case studies, reports, research, release notes, help content, documentation, agent receipts.

### Confidence and epistemic status

Confidence is **high**. All five sources use literal or attributed proof close to important claims. Epistemic status is **strong inference**.

### Originality risk

Risk is **medium** at the structural level and **high** if source-specific interfaces are copied. Mitigation requires real Mez content, correct evidence-class labels, provenance, and a rule that staged UI cannot be presented as an outcome.

### Tension or contradiction

- Literal proof improves credibility but can overload small channels.
- Interactive proof is strongest at evaluation time, while ads and email need static transformations that retain evidence without pretending to be live.
- First-party figures can demonstrate attribution structure without proving methodology or causality.

### Candidate experiments

- `E-03` Claim-proof transformation.
- `E-05` Semantic object portability pilot for the proof record and validator.

**Decision status:** `UNAPPROVED`.

## `M-04` Semantic density

**Research questions:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-03`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-08`  
**Problem:** One universal section rhythm cannot serve calm executive marketing, operational interfaces, compact promotion, lifecycle communication, and analytical reporting without either losing truth or producing clutter.

### Source observations

- **Notion:** low-density orientation leads into denser product evaluation; block adjacency compresses lists while preserving paragraph breathing room. Evidence: `NTN-E14`, `NTN-E15`, `NTN-E16`, `NTN-E17`.
- **Linear:** calm narrative opens into dense work states, while directories, docs, and changelogs use task-specific scanning and detail. Reduced shell competition makes dense content usable. Evidence: `LIN-E09`, `LIN-E10`.
- **Stripe:** documentation, reporting, marketing, and editorial pages use different information structures while retaining the parent chassis. Evidence: `STRIPE-E-009`, `STRIPE-E-010`, `STRIPE-E-011`.
- **ElevenLabs:** product pages concentrate controls and output context where quality is evaluated, while documentation moves from portfolio paths to shared concepts and implementation detail. Evidence: `ELV-E11`, `ELV-E12`.
- **Ramp:** product marketing, Economics Lab cards, dated analysis, and report libraries allocate density according to mechanism, metric, method, and archive needs. Evidence: `RAMP-E-007`, `RAMP-E-008`, `RAMP-E-009`, `RAMP-E-010`.

### Cross-source mechanism

Density follows receiver intent, decision depth, information object, neighbour relationship, and inspection time. The evidence order can remain stable while the amount, granularity, and interaction depth change.

### Transferable principle

Define Mez density modes for executive calm, operational inspection, compact promotion, lifecycle communication, and analytical reporting. Each mode must declare minimum retained truth, permitted compression, hierarchy, grouping, and accessibility behaviour.

### Exact expressions to avoid

- Notion's exact block spacing, property pills, list chunking, and editorial-to-database composition.
- Linear's dimmed sidebars, dense inbox, changelog layout, directory cards, and thin-bordered dark shell.
- Stripe's documentation rails, ruled article grid, annual-letter frame, and exact report navigation.
- ElevenLabs' media-player stacks, voice selectors, prompt panels, language controls, and progressive documentation layout.
- Ramp's Economics Lab cards, miniature trend charts, report archive, and finance-product matrix.

### Scope and channels

Marketing, product UI, dashboards, ads, social, email, reports, decks, case studies, documentation, release notes, templates, mobile and narrow surfaces.

### Confidence and epistemic status

Confidence is **high** for the principle and **medium** for exact channel limits, which require Mez content testing. Epistemic status is **strong inference**.

### Originality risk

Risk is **medium**. Density rules are broadly transferable, but source-specific page rhythms and shells are not. Mitigation requires using identical Mez proof content across modes and evaluating information loss rather than visual similarity.

### Tension or contradiction

- Calm framing improves orientation, but too much restraint can hide the literal proof needed for credibility.
- Dense evidence improves inspection, but shell and control competition can make the system feel noisy.
- Deterministic templates improve reliability, but fixed compositions cannot respond to different information objects and channels.

### Candidate experiments

- `E-01` Monochrome recognition test across five density contexts.
- `E-03` Claim-proof transformation across five channel modes.

**Decision status:** `UNAPPROVED`.

## `M-05` Motion as state meaning

**Research questions:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-06`, `RQ-08`  
**Problem:** Ambient particles, glowing waves, orbs, and arbitrary movement can signal fashionable AI styling without communicating state, intelligence, orchestration, or responsibility.

### Source observations

- **Linear:** public proof is organised around routing, progress, status change, roadmap stage, diff comparison, project health, and live agent activity. Evidence: `LIN-E11`.
- **ElevenLabs:** pause, play, speaking, waiting, timeline position, synchronisation, generation, permission, interruption, handoff, and continuation are meaningful product states. Evidence: `ELV-E03`, `ELV-E10`, `ELV-E13`, `ELV-E14`.
- **Notion:** agent runs, reversibility, and changed workspace states support a broader state vocabulary, but animation behaviour was not measured. Evidence: `NTN-E12`, `NTN-E20`.
- **Ramp:** live-looking activity and changing metrics suggest state display, but cadence and animation truth were not verified. Evidence: `RAMP-E-002`.

### Cross-source mechanism

Useful motion communicates a meaningful transition in progress, ownership, permission, transformation, synchronisation, exception, handoff, verification, completion, or reversal. Static and reduced-motion equivalents preserve the same state meaning.

### Transferable principle

Define the shared Mez state vocabulary before specifying visual motion. Each product may express a state through its own information objects, but ownership, status, accessibility, and reduced-motion meaning must remain common.

### Exact expressions to avoid

- Linear's exact agent progress, issue routing, diff comparison, roadmap animation, and motion timing.
- ElevenLabs' pause and play symbolism, waveforms, voice orbs, timeline and playback motion, Chladni response, and kinetic type.
- Notion's agent characters, workspace animation, and specific reversible-action presentations.
- Ramp's ticker motion, counter cadence, dotted field, and any representation that implies live data without verified freshness.
- Generic AI particles, pulsing spheres, travelling gradients, light sweeps, and decorative wave motion without a state contract.

### Scope and channels

Product UI, web demonstrations, launch films, social video, microinteraction, loading and progress, handoff, verification, error and recovery, static storyboards, reduced-motion output.

### Confidence and epistemic status

Confidence is **medium**. State semantics are well supported, but duration, easing, choreography, responsiveness, and reduced-motion implementations were not measured. Epistemic status is **tentative inference**.

### Originality risk

Risk is **high** because audio, agent, and workflow motion signatures are strongly attributable. Mitigation requires starting from Mez state semantics, testing static frames first, and prohibiting direct source metaphors.

### Tension or contradiction

- Shared state semantics improve family coherence, but identical motion across four products would erase product character.
- Rich motion can clarify transformations, but reduced-motion and static outputs must remain equally comprehensible.
- Live-looking activity increases immediacy, but uncertain cadence or synthetic counters can damage trust.

### Candidate experiments

- `E-04` Orchestration-state storyboard.

**Decision status:** `UNAPPROVED`.

## `M-06` Portable semantic objects

**Research questions:** `RQ-02`, `RQ-07`, `RQ-08`  
**Problem:** Figma-only truth, prose-only guidance, duplicated tokens, and hidden prompt context create drift between design, code, templates, humans, and LLM-generated outputs.

### Source observations

- **Notion:** typed blocks and relationships support human editing and systematic layout; API connections expose capabilities, access, authentication, and permissioned objects. Evidence: `NTN-E18`, `NTN-E19`, `NTN-E20`.
- **Linear:** a public operating method, stable product objects, explicit integration actions, and whole-system governance preserve language outside the interface. Evidence: `LIN-E12`, `LIN-E13`, `LIN-E14`.
- **Stripe:** semantic components, Figma availability, controlled tokens, styling constraints, LLM copy, and Markdown expose one bounded extension system to several consumers. Evidence: `STRIPE-E-006`, `STRIPE-E-007`, `STRIPE-E-008`, `STRIPE-E-009`.
- **ElevenLabs:** explicit naming rules, stable concept identifiers, shared models, APIs, SDKs, roles, approvals, and audit concepts transfer capability across no-code and code surfaces. Evidence: `ELV-E15`, `ELV-E16`, `ELV-E17`.
- **Ramp:** report series, release taxonomies, help definitions, and data-representation rules publish stable operational nouns and reuse constraints. Evidence: `RAMP-E-010`, `RAMP-E-011`, `RAMP-E-012`, `RAMP-E-013`.

### Cross-source mechanism

Transfer succeeds when objects, actions, states, permissions, IDs, required content, constraints, allowed transformations, prohibited transformations, and validation rules are explicit. Figma, code, documentation, templates, and prompts become consumers of one semantic registry rather than independent authorities.

### Transferable principle

Create one versioned Mez semantic registry. Every reusable object must declare its stable ID, purpose, supported claims, required evidence, content slots, states, accessibility, permissions, channel adaptations, allowed transformations, prohibited transformations, provenance, deprecation status, code API, Figma mapping, LLM context, and validator.

### Exact expressions to avoid

- Notion's block taxonomy, slash command, API object naming, database model, and page composition.
- Linear's Method language, issue and cycle taxonomy, integration categories, and exact numbered doctrine.
- Stripe's component names, token names, documentation layout, app indicator, copy-for-LLM interaction, and extension constraints as written.
- ElevenLabs' voice IDs, model names, credit model, platform naming, SDK examples, and approval terminology.
- Ramp's finance object names, release categories, report taxonomy, data definitions, and partnership representation language.

### Scope and channels

Semantic registry, Figma libraries, code packages, design tokens, content schemas, templates, HTML references, documentation, prompt fragments, agent context, validators, migration and deprecation tooling.

### Confidence and epistemic status

Confidence is **high** for the need for a shared semantic contract. Stripe provides the strongest public multi-format implementation evidence. Other sources reinforce object stability but do not prove their private Figma-to-code architecture. Epistemic status is **strong inference**.

### Originality risk

Risk is **medium**. Semantic architecture is transferable, but copying source taxonomies or public component models would import their product logic. Mitigation requires Mez-native nouns, independent object modelling, schema review, and cross-renderer tests.

### Tension or contradiction

- Deterministic semantics improve reliability, but unlimited rigidity would prevent channel-appropriate composition.
- Human guidance permits judgement, while machine validation needs explicit fields and stop conditions.
- One registry reduces drift, but ownership, versioning, deprecation, and migration become programme-level obligations.

### Candidate experiments

- `E-02` Four-product territory matrix encoded as shared and product-owned semantic fields.
- `E-05` Semantic object portability pilot.

**Decision status:** `UNAPPROVED`.

## `M-07` Control and provenance are part of the design

**Research questions:** `RQ-03`, `RQ-06`, `RQ-07`, `RQ-08`  
**Problem:** LLM-first outputs can appear polished while hiding their source, status, permissions, uncertainty, changes, review point, or recovery path.

### Source observations

- **Notion:** agent proof includes permissions, logged runs, audit trails, reversible changes, admin controls, and explicit access boundaries. Evidence: `NTN-E12`, `NTN-E18`, `NTN-E19`, `NTN-E20`.
- **Linear:** agent work shows assignment, repository context, changed artefacts, intermediate state, and human handoff. Evidence: `LIN-E06`, `LIN-E07`.
- **Stripe:** extensions are bounded by accessibility and styling constraints, while documentation exposes semantic contracts and LLM-readable forms. Evidence: `STRIPE-E-006`, `STRIPE-E-007`, `STRIPE-E-008`, `STRIPE-E-009`.
- **ElevenLabs:** agent and creative proof includes guardrails, testing, roles, approvals, audit logs, permission modes, human interruption, and continuation from edited state. Evidence: `ELV-E09`, `ELV-E10`, `ELV-E14`, `ELV-E17`.
- **Ramp:** research and data distribution expose dates, authorship, definitions, methodology paths, citation, and accurate-representation constraints. Evidence: `RAMP-E-007`, `RAMP-E-008`, `RAMP-E-009`, `RAMP-E-010`, `RAMP-E-013`.

### Cross-source mechanism

Trust controls are visible parts of the experience. Source, evidence class, permission, current owner, run history, affected artefact, uncertainty, audit state, review, reversal, and representation constraints shape what the receiver can believe and do.

### Transferable principle

Every Mez-generated output and system action should expose provenance, status, permission, verification, uncertainty, human decision, and recovery according to risk. These must be designed as reusable visual and interaction primitives linked to governance records.

### Exact expressions to avoid

- Notion's exact agent-run UI, permission panels, admin controls, audit language, and workspace imagery.
- Linear's session activity, repository and diff treatment, issue assignment, review states, and agent-launch controls.
- Stripe's extension permission, app indicator, documentation controls, and LLM-copy interface.
- ElevenLabs' guardrail interface, simulation outputs, latency displays, plan-and-create modes, and timeline handoff.
- Ramp's Economics Lab identity, methodology-card format, data-partner language, citations, charts, and first-party metrics.

### Scope and channels

Agent runs, generated websites, ads, email, reports, design outputs, product UI, receipts, review panels, change logs, exports, research claims, data visualisation, template and component generation.

### Confidence and epistemic status

Confidence is **high** for high-risk AI, claim, and data outputs. The required level of visible control should vary by risk and channel. Epistemic status is **strong inference**.

### Originality risk

Risk is **medium**. Governance concepts are broadly transferable, but source-specific control surfaces are not. Mitigation requires a Mez risk model, original visual grammar, minimum disclosure rules, and usability testing with receivers.

### Tension or contradiction

- Visible controls improve trust, but too much governance metadata can overwhelm low-risk creative outputs.
- Human review preserves taste and accountability, but excessive gating defeats the low-burden operating goal.
- Reversibility is critical for system actions, while static creative exports need version and source recovery rather than an in-product undo control.

### Candidate experiments

- `E-03` Claim-proof transformation with evidence class and provenance.
- `E-04` Orchestration-state storyboard with ownership, exception, handoff, and reversal.
- `E-05` Semantic object portability pilot with receipts and validation.

**Decision status:** `UNAPPROVED`.

## Promotion rule

No row in this matrix is a Mez token, component, pattern, visual territory, motion specification, or brand decision. Promotion requires an original Mez problem statement, a prototype using real Mez content, evidence-linked evaluation, source-signature review, accessibility review, the relevant experiment pass condition, and a recorded human decision at H2 or later.
