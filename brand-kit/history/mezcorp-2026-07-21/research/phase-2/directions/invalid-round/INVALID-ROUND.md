# TR-6 direction round invalidation

Status: invalid research round  
Date: 20 July 2026  
Production authority: none  
Preference authority: none beyond the explicit observations below

## Blunt verdict

The three routes were not credible, production-quality Mez visions. They were broad style demonstrations assembled across too many surfaces at once. The exercise therefore measured Olli's reaction to weak execution and off-system styling, not preference between viable directions.

The exported record is preserved as evidence but its three `donor` verdicts must not be aggregated as route support. All three routes had zero retained behaviours, and the review was followed immediately by a clear rejection of the artefact quality and decision format.

## Explicit valid evidence from Olli

- Moderately rounded buttons are positive. Square buttons are strongly negative. Extreme rounding is not automatically preferred.
- Square, border-defined boxes without rounded corners are strongly negative.
- Forced serif typography is strongly negative. Keep the main visual language simple and sans-first.
- `Turn a request into verified work` is rejected as a headline.
- Kinetic intelligence was the least-wrong route, not an approved direction.
- Kinetic intelligence surface 02, the product UI, was the strongest visual in the round.
- The Kinetic product-family idea had some potential, but the square boxed execution undermined it.
- The colour choices felt out of control and disconnected from Mez.
- The review did not make the vision legible enough for a confident human decision.

## Why the process failed

### 1. It converted evidence into adjectives, then adjectives into costumes

The source review produced useful qualities such as premium, clear, intelligent and product-led. The direction brief then treated those qualities as permission to invent three visually distant genres. That translation was not traceable enough to exact source behaviours or Mez requirements.

### 2. It broke locked Mez rules to manufacture contrast

The current system is Inter-first, uses serif only as an occasional accent, has 20px cards and 28px panels, and limits colour to product gradients. The prototypes introduced new orange, blue, red, warm-paper and black values; used serif 23 times; and made most B and C surfaces square. These were avoidable violations, not legitimate tests.

### 3. It spread effort across 24 outputs before proving one

Three routes multiplied by eight surfaces created 24 compositions. This produced breadth without enough art direction, content craft, responsive refinement or product realism. A weak website, ad, email and deck do not collectively become a strong vision.

### 4. It bundled unrelated decisions

The review mixed copy, colour, typography, radius, page composition, product UI, family architecture, advertising and motion. Disliking a headline or a square card contaminated judgement of the entire route. The resulting verdict could not identify the actual cause.

### 5. It used a contact-sheet review for vision approval

The designs were shown as long, reduced-scale pages with eight different artefacts. That format is useful for coverage checks, not for seeing craft, interaction, hierarchy or a believable finished brand. It asked for a holistic decision without providing a holistic experience.

### 6. It skipped an internal quality gate

The round should never have reached Olli while obvious system violations, generic copy and wireframe-level surfaces remained. Technical validation proved completeness and authority boundaries, but it did not prove visual quality.

## Reset rules

1. Do not ask Olli to compare three more broad art directions.
2. Start from the strongest observed anchor: the Kinetic product UI.
3. Build one coherent Mez evolution, not a new genre.
4. Use only current Mez colour tokens and product gradients until a realistic artefact proves a need to change them.
5. Use Inter for the next build. Do not use serif merely to signal premium or editorial quality.
6. Give primary interactive controls moderate corners. Start at 10 to 12px for review. Reserve pills for compact tags or explicitly pill-shaped controls.
7. Give cards and major panels clear rounding. Do not use square border boxes as the main composition language.
8. Separate copy approval from visual approval. Do not reuse the rejected headline.
9. Prove one flagship desktop product screen and its mobile state before expanding.
10. Present work at full size with a short design rationale and a small number of concrete annotation prompts.

## Replacement sequence

### Gate A: one product anchor

Build a production-grade AI OS product screen based on the useful parts of Kinetic surface 02. Use realistic hierarchy, content, states, controls and responsive behaviour. Review only whether the product feels like Mez and which concrete details are wrong.

### Gate B: one brand-to-product bridge

After the product anchor is credible, build one website hero and one product-family module using the same visual grammar. Use current or already approved copy. Review them full-size, not as a contact sheet.

### Gate C: system extraction

Extract the successful radius, type, surface, colour, component and composition rules into tokens and named patterns. Identify where the existing locked system must be amended rather than bypassing it in prototype CSS.

### Gate D: transfer

Only after the anchor and bridge pass should the system expand to ad, email, deck, report, identity and motion. Each output must be channel-native and independently art-directed.

### Gate E: controlled comparisons

Use side-by-side comparisons only for genuinely unresolved variables, such as 10px versus 14px control radius. Both options must be production-worthy and all unrelated variables must remain fixed.

## Human involvement

Olli should review one credible artefact at each gate for approximately five to ten minutes. The review should ask for direct annotations such as `keep`, `change`, `too much`, `too plain`, and `not Mez`, with an optional note. It must not force a route winner or ask Olli to approve abstract adjectives.
