# Mez Systems anti-pattern atlas

Status: `UNAPPROVED_RESEARCH`  
Prepared: 20 July 2026  
Decision gate: H2 for visual territories, H3 for motion and hierarchy, H4 for transfer operations  
Evidence panel: Notion, Linear, Stripe, ElevenLabs, Ramp

## Purpose and limitation

This atlas turns observed failure risks into testable design checks. It is not a moodboard, token source, or list of styles to avoid by instinct. Each item identifies a failure mechanism, a bounded exception, and a repair that must be tested with Mez content.

The panel is deliberately narrow and contains five polished software brands. It has no dedicated anti-reference, limited cross-category evidence, incomplete ad and email evidence, incomplete small-size evidence, and weak measured motion evidence. This atlas therefore does not replace dedicated anti-reference research. It records provisional risks exposed by the current Mez audit and the five-source comparison. New anti-reference work may add, split, revise, or retire these entries.

## Use

- Apply the atlas during concept review, prototype critique, LLM evaluation, and pre-production validation.
- A matching signal is a reason to inspect, not an automatic rejection.
- Exceptions require a named purpose, evidence, and reviewer. Familiarity or polish is not an exception.
- Repair the underlying information, proof, state, or transfer problem before changing surface styling.
- No entry approves a replacement design direction.

## Atlas

### `AP-001` Single-device recognition

**Pattern:** The identity depends on the wings, product name, gradient core, disc, or one other obvious device. Remove that device and the output becomes generic software.

**Why it fails Mez:** Mez must stay recognisable across interfaces, reports, email, ads, small placements, code-generated outputs, and monochrome contexts. One device cannot survive every constraint or carry every product job.

**Signals:** Logo enlarged to rescue weak composition; gradient repeated in every focal area; neutralised mockups become indistinguishable; no recurring evidence order, relationship geometry, annotation, state language, or density behaviour.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-01`; `NTN-E01` to `NTN-E03`; `LIN-E01` to `LIN-E03`; `STRIPE-E-001`, `STRIPE-E-002`; `ELV-E01` to `ELV-E03`; `RAMP-E-001`, `RAMP-E-002`.

**Exceptions:** A favicon, avatar, legal mark placement, or constrained product tile may legitimately use one compact identifier after the broader system is already established.

**Repair strategy:** Define at least four independent non-colour invariants across composition, evidence framing, relationship geometry, type hierarchy, state language, and motion purpose. Let the mark and gradient reinforce them.

**Originality risk:** Medium. Distributed recognition is a principle, but any source's exact grid, inline object, numbering system, diagonal, playback symbol, or activity ticker is attributable.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-05`.

**Validation test:** Run E-01 with names, wings, gradients, and product colours removed. Reviewers should group the Mez family above chance and should not identify one source brand as the template.

### `AP-002` Gradient as intelligence

**Pattern:** A glow, gradient, iridescent field, orb, particle cloud, or bright colour shift is used as the primary evidence that a system is intelligent.

**Why it fails Mez:** Atmosphere cannot show what the system did, what changed, what evidence exists, or where human control sits. Mez already has strong gradient equity, so overuse converts a useful product cue into generic AI decoration.

**Signals:** Abstract light behind an unsupported claim; motion with no state change; every product uses the same luminous treatment; proof disappears when the background is removed.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-03`, `M-05`, and `RQ-08`; `LIN-E06`, `LIN-E07`, `LIN-E11`; `ELV-E07` to `ELV-E14`; `RAMP-E-002` to `RAMP-E-004`.

**Exceptions:** A product core may identify a product, establish campaign emotion, or punctuate a transition when literal proof and state meaning remain present elsewhere in the unit.

**Repair strategy:** Replace atmospheric implication with an input, action, changed state, result, provenance marker, or control. Use the gradient only in its approved product role.

**Originality risk:** High. Iridescent ribbons, audio orbs, Chladni patterns, luminous spheres, and specific colour territories are source signatures as well as saturated category devices.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-03`, `RQ-06`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** Convert the concept to flat monochrome. A reviewer must still identify the system action, current state, result, and product territory without explanatory copy.

### `AP-003` Dark developer-tool cosplay

**Pattern:** Dark surfaces, fine borders, sparse type, purple-blue atmosphere, terminal fragments, or code decoration are used as a shortcut to technical credibility.

**Why it fails Mez:** Forge is technical, but the suite also includes advertising, analytics, and business orchestration. A borrowed developer-tool mood narrows the company, weakens product distinction, and imitates category appearance without operational rigour.

**Signals:** Decorative code that cannot run; empty command bars; low-contrast chrome; darkness applied to every product; claims supported only by technical-looking fragments.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative conventions, `RQ-08`, and Linear's useful warning; `LIN-E06`, `LIN-E07`, `LIN-E10`; `STRIPE-E-004`; `RAMP-E-004`.

**Exceptions:** A real code, diff, log, or terminal state may be shown where it is the literal object of work and meets accessibility requirements.

**Repair strategy:** Start with the real object, state transition, provenance, and output. Choose surface tone from the task and channel, not from a presumed technical aesthetic.

**Originality risk:** High when the result resembles Linear or another dark developer platform. Medium when a real Forge workflow determines the composition.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-02`, `RQ-03`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** Remove colour and code styling. If the workflow cannot be understood from objects, states, and relationships, reject the concept.

### `AP-004` Colour-swapped product family

**Pattern:** AI OS, Aurora, Prism, and Forge share one layout, one proof style, and one campaign posture. Only name, copy, screenshot, and gradient change.

**Why it fails Mez:** The products have different users, jobs, information objects, outputs, failure states, and proof needs. Cosmetic variation makes them reskins and forces colour to do semantic work it cannot reliably perform.

**Signals:** Identical hero, feature order, cards, density, motion, and result treatment; product identification fails in monochrome; any screenshot can be swapped between products without structural change.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-02` and `RQ-02`; `NTN-E04` to `NTN-E08`; `LIN-E04`, `LIN-E05`; `STRIPE-E-003`, `STRIPE-E-005`; `ELV-E04` to `ELV-E06`; `RAMP-E-004` to `RAMP-E-006`.

**Exceptions:** Parent navigation, accessibility, core controls, evidence anatomy, and semantic state names should remain shared. Commodity commerce or legal surfaces may intentionally converge.

**Repair strategy:** Give each product a territory contract covering user, job, dominant object, proof primitive, result type, density, motion emphasis, control state, and campaign posture.

**Originality risk:** Medium. The repair is a portfolio principle, but copying Notion's objects, Stripe's local rail, ElevenLabs' platform palettes, or Ramp's product matrix remains prohibited.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-02`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-05`, `RQ-06`.

**Validation test:** Run E-02 as a monochrome, nameless four-product contact sheet. Each product must remain distinct by job and proof while still reading as one family.

### `AP-005` One universal page template

**Pattern:** Every product and channel inherits the same fixed section order, card geometry, information density, and CTA rhythm because reuse is mistaken for consistency.

**Why it fails Mez:** Deterministic generation needs rules, not identical pages. Fixed structure suppresses product truth and makes reports, email, ads, product UI, and editorial content feel like resized landing pages.

**Signals:** Repeated hero-feature-grid-testimonial-CTA sequence; empty sections retained to preserve a template; report evidence placed in marketing cards; email duplicates website hierarchy.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-04`, `M-06`, and tension 4; `NTN-E14` to `NTN-E18`; `LIN-E09`, `LIN-E10`; `STRIPE-E-009` to `STRIPE-E-011`; `ELV-E11`, `ELV-E12`; `RAMP-E-007` to `RAMP-E-010`.

**Exceptions:** Checkout, legal, receipt, and other risk-sensitive flows may use highly controlled archetypes. Shared evidence order can remain invariant while presentation depth changes.

**Repair strategy:** Encode semantic objects, required fields, adjacency rules, density modes, and allowed transformations. Let each channel compose the minimum truth required for its receiver.

**Originality risk:** Low for semantic reuse, high if the replacement simply copies a source's page architecture.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-02`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-07`.

**Validation test:** Transform one proof record across website, product UI, ad, email, and report. Require semantic equivalence without identical layout.

### `AP-006` Decorative interface proof

**Pattern:** A browser frame, dashboard crop, device mockup, or floating UI card sits beside a claim but does not show the mechanism named by that claim.

**Why it fails Mez:** It creates the appearance of product evidence while hiding whether the product can perform the promised work. It also encourages fabricated interfaces that drift from production.

**Signals:** Unreadable or empty UI; a generic dashboard reused for several claims; staged data with no source or state; the claim and screenshot describe different objects.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-03` and `RQ-03`; `NTN-E09` to `NTN-E13`; `LIN-E06` to `LIN-E08`; `STRIPE-E-004`; `ELV-E07` to `ELV-E10`; `RAMP-E-004`.

**Exceptions:** A clearly labelled conceptual diagram may explain architecture when a literal product state is unavailable or would reveal sensitive data. It must not be presented as a live interface.

**Repair strategy:** Use one inspectable proof object per claim. Show the input, action, state change, output, and relevant control. Label staged, conceptual, or representative material.

**Originality risk:** Medium. Literal proof is transferable, but source screenshots, payment forms, database views, voice players, finance objects, and their compositions are excluded.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-03`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** Ask a reviewer to identify exactly which visual element proves the claim and which state changed. Failure to point to both is a rejection.

### `AP-007` Evidence-class substitution

**Pattern:** A logo, quote, live-looking counter, product demo, customer outcome, or aggregate statistic is used as if it proves a different evidence class.

**Why it fails Mez:** Trust collapses when system activity impersonates research, a staged UI impersonates a customer result, or a testimonial impersonates a mechanism. LLM-generated outputs amplify this risk by recombining claims and proof.

**Signals:** Uncited numbers; no date or method; customer logos beside product capability claims; causal language applied to correlation; telemetry without provenance.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-03`, `M-07`, and candidate proof record; `NTN-E12`; `LIN-E07`, `LIN-E08`; `STRIPE-E-004`, `STRIPE-E-012`; `ELV-E09`, `ELV-E10`; `RAMP-E-002`, `RAMP-E-003`, `RAMP-E-008`, `RAMP-E-009`, `RAMP-E-013`.

**Exceptions:** Compact channels may show one evidence class only if it is correctly labelled and resolves to fuller provenance.

**Repair strategy:** Assign every claim one declared class: system activity, literal mechanism, customer outcome, or aggregate research. Require the matching fields and prohibit cross-class inference.

**Originality risk:** Low. This is evidence governance, not visual borrowing.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-03`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-07`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** A validator and human reviewer must trace each public claim to its evidence class, source, date, method where relevant, and approval status.

### `AP-008` Magical agent with hidden control

**Pattern:** AI is shown as instant, autonomous, and infallible while permissions, intermediate state, changed artefact, exceptions, human review, and rollback remain invisible.

**Why it fails Mez:** Mez outputs are received by humans and may affect real work. Concealed agency makes the product less credible, increases risk, and prevents a reviewer from knowing what to trust or change.

**Signals:** One-click transformation with no preview; chat bubble as the entire proof; no source references; no owner or approval state; no exception or reversal path.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-05`, `M-07`; `NTN-E12`, `NTN-E18` to `NTN-E20`; `LIN-E07`; `ELV-E09`, `ELV-E10`, `ELV-E14`, `ELV-E17`; `RAMP-E-009`, `RAMP-E-013`.

**Exceptions:** Low-risk, reversible micro-actions may collapse controls after the user establishes permission and the system preserves an accessible history.

**Repair strategy:** Expose origin, current owner, action boundary, intermediate state, affected artefact, confidence or exception, verification, human decision, and reversal where appropriate.

**Originality risk:** Low for the governance principle. High if using a source's exact run log, agent session, timeline, or handoff composition.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-03`, `RQ-06`, `RQ-07`.

**Validation test:** Storyboard success and recoverable failure. A reviewer must identify who owns each step, what changed, what remains uncertain, and how to stop or reverse it.

### `AP-009` Ambient motion without state meaning

**Pattern:** Particles, waves, glowing paths, kinetic type, floating cards, or looping parallax move continuously without communicating progress, ownership, permission, change, or completion.

**Why it fails Mez:** Decorative motion increases noise, weakens accessibility, consumes attention, and repeats saturated AI signals without explaining orchestration.

**Signals:** Loop continues when the system is idle; the same animation represents success and failure; motion has no static equivalent; removing animation loses no information.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-05`, `RQ-06`, and `RQ-08`; `LIN-E11`; `ELV-E03`, `ELV-E10`, `ELV-E13`, `ELV-E14`.

**Exceptions:** Brief atmospheric motion may establish campaign emotion if it is secondary, optional, performance-safe, and reduced-motion compliant.

**Repair strategy:** Define shared states first: idle, preparing, active, waiting, needs input, changed, verified, exception, handed off, complete, and reversible. Animate only meaningful transitions.

**Originality risk:** High for audio-derived waves, playback symbols, orbs, source-specific kinetic type, and familiar AI particle systems.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-06`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** Show static start and end frames plus reduced-motion treatment. Reviewers must infer state, ownership, and outcome without the full animation.

### `AP-010` One density everywhere

**Pattern:** Every surface is either uniformly sparse or uniformly dense, regardless of receiver, decision depth, information object, or inspection time.

**Why it fails Mez:** Sparse product UI hides action and evidence. Dense ads and executive pages block orientation. Consistency becomes sameness rather than controlled information compression.

**Signals:** Identical spacing and content count in hero, dashboard, ad, email, and report; tiny report text; oversized operational controls; missing method or source in analytical output.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-04` and `RQ-04`; `NTN-E14` to `NTN-E17`; `LIN-E09`, `LIN-E10`; `STRIPE-E-009` to `STRIPE-E-011`; `ELV-E11`, `ELV-E12`; `RAMP-E-007` to `RAMP-E-010`.

**Exceptions:** A campaign series may deliberately hold a narrow density band for recognition, provided product and report surfaces retain their task-specific modes.

**Repair strategy:** Define executive calm, operational, compact promotion, lifecycle, and analytical modes. Preserve evidence order while changing depth, controls, and annotation.

**Originality risk:** Low. Density modulation is a system principle.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-02`, `RQ-04`.

**Validation test:** Use the same source record across all five modes. Check scan path, minimum retained truth, accessibility, and time to identify the next action.

### `AP-011` Rounded-card monoculture

**Pattern:** Every content type becomes a rounded card in a repeated grid, including evidence, features, testimonials, products, metrics, workflow states, and reports.

**Why it fails Mez:** It erases relationships between unlike objects, creates interchangeable products, and turns the system into a generic SaaS kit. Container consistency replaces information design.

**Signals:** Same radius, padding, and hierarchy for every object; grids used where sequence or dependency matters; cards nested inside cards; product family represented only as four equal tiles.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative category conventions and `RQ-08`; `NTN-E03`, `NTN-E16`; `LIN-E04`, `LIN-E09`; `RAMP-E-004` to `RAMP-E-006`.

**Exceptions:** Cards are appropriate for genuinely discrete, reorderable peers such as integrations, product choices, or compact summaries.

**Repair strategy:** Choose structure from the relationship: sequence, comparison, hierarchy, dependency, timeline, evidence chain, or discrete collection. Use containers only where they express that relationship.

**Originality risk:** Medium. Generic cards are saturated, while copying a source's specific block, product matrix, or integration grid is high risk.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-02`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** Label every container with its semantic relationship. If the relationship is only `looks organised`, redesign it.

### `AP-012` Source-signature collage

**Pattern:** A direction combines admired source signatures, then replaces their logos, colours, or copy with Mez branding.

**Why it fails Mez:** The result may look polished but cannot become ownable. It also creates inconsistent rules because each borrowed expression came from a different operating model.

**Signals:** Descriptions such as Notion typography plus Linear dark mode plus Stripe gradients plus ElevenLabs motion plus Ramp proof; a reviewer can name the source from one composition; source tokens or measurements appear in Mez files.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Every memo's source-signature exclusion list; comparative `What should not happen next`; research stop criteria.

**Exceptions:** Abstract mechanisms may transfer after combination with a direct Mez requirement, original translation, prototype, validation, and human decision.

**Repair strategy:** Begin from a Mez problem and operating model. Combine multiple principles at the mechanism level, then run an authorship and anti-copy review before promotion.

**Originality risk:** Critical.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-02`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** Ask independent reviewers what brand the direction resembles. Any repeated source attribution triggers redesign, even if individual assets are technically original.

### `AP-013` Figma-only or prose-only truth

**Pattern:** The canonical decision exists only in a Figma canvas, a Markdown paragraph, a code component, or an LLM prompt, with manually maintained mirrors elsewhere.

**Why it fails Mez:** Humans and LLMs consume different surfaces. Manual duplication creates drift, stale variants, inconsistent naming, and outputs that look similar while behaving differently.

**Signals:** No stable IDs; token values copied by hand; component names differ between Figma and code; prompts include hidden rules; deprecated patterns remain discoverable; no validator.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-06` and `RQ-07`; `NTN-E18` to `NTN-E20`; `LIN-E12` to `LIN-E14`; `STRIPE-E-006` to `STRIPE-E-009`; `ELV-E15` to `ELV-E17`; `RAMP-E-010` to `RAMP-E-013`.

**Exceptions:** Early concept exploration may live in one medium before approval. It must be labelled exploratory and cannot be used as production truth.

**Repair strategy:** Create one versioned semantic registry with IDs, purpose, content requirements, state, provenance, allowed transformations, prohibited transformations, mappings, deprecation, and tests.

**Originality risk:** Low. The risk is operational drift rather than imitation.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-02`, `RQ-05`, `RQ-07`.

**Validation test:** Give the same registry entry to one human and two LLMs through different supported surfaces. Outputs must be semantically equivalent and report the same version and provenance.

### `AP-014` Channel truth drift

**Pattern:** Website, ad, email, product UI, and report versions of the same claim use different mechanisms, results, qualifiers, or evidence because each channel is authored independently.

**Why it fails Mez:** Transferable identity depends on semantic consistency, not identical layout. Claim drift creates legal, trust, and product accuracy risk, especially in LLM-led production.

**Signals:** Ad promises more than product; email changes evidence class; report cannot trace the campaign metric; screenshots or product names differ; qualifiers disappear in compact formats.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-03`, `M-04`, `M-06`, and E-03; `NTN-E13`, `NTN-E17` to `NTN-E20`; `LIN-E05`, `LIN-E08`; `STRIPE-E-009`, `STRIPE-E-010`; `ELV-E12`, `ELV-E16`; `RAMP-E-010` to `RAMP-E-013`.

**Exceptions:** Channel copy may change tone and depth when it resolves to the same canonical claim and retains required qualifiers.

**Repair strategy:** Store one structured proof record. Let each channel reveal an approved subset and require links back to source evidence.

**Originality risk:** Low.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-03`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-07`.

**Validation test:** Diff the extracted claim, mechanism, result, qualifier, source, and status fields across five channel outputs. No unauthorised semantic change may remain.

### `AP-015` Restraint without an invariant

**Pattern:** Neutral colour, sparse type, generous whitespace, thin borders, and quiet cards are assumed to create premium identity without a distinctive operating behaviour.

**Why it fails Mez:** Restraint can improve hierarchy but is common across mature software. Without repeated evidence, relationship, or state grammar, the result is tasteful and anonymous.

**Signals:** Design is described only as clean, premium, minimal, or calm; no distinctive rule survives monochrome; product proof is reduced to maintain emptiness; accessibility suffers from faint contrast.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative tension 1, conventions, and `RQ-01`; `NTN-E01` to `NTN-E03`; `LIN-E01` to `LIN-E03`, `LIN-E10`; `STRIPE-E-001`, `STRIPE-E-002`.

**Exceptions:** Legal, checkout, receipt, and high-trust surfaces may prioritise clarity and restraint over expressive recognition, while retaining essential parent and evidence cues.

**Repair strategy:** Pair restraint with Mez-specific composition, evidence, relationship, and state invariants. Increase contrast or density where the task requires inspection.

**Originality risk:** Medium. Minimal software styling is saturated and easily drifts towards Linear, Notion, or Stripe.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-04`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** Blind-test a monochrome specimen beside generic premium SaaS outputs. Reviewers must identify a recurring Mez behaviour, not merely prefer its polish.

### `AP-016` Proof overload

**Pattern:** Every claim, state, source, control, caveat, and chart is shown at once in the name of credibility.

**Why it fails Mez:** Literal proof improves trust only when the receiver can orient, inspect, and act. Undifferentiated completeness makes executive pages, ads, and email unreadable and hides the primary decision.

**Signals:** More than one focal proof object; identical detail in hero and report; methods crowd out the claim; no progressive disclosure; all evidence has equal contrast.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-04`, tension 3, and `RQ-04`; `NTN-E14` to `NTN-E17`; `LIN-E09`, `LIN-E10`; `ELV-E11`, `ELV-E12`; `RAMP-E-007` to `RAMP-E-010`.

**Exceptions:** Analytical reports and operational inspection views may be dense when hierarchy, filtering, export, and source access support the task.

**Repair strategy:** Preserve the evidence chain but modulate depth. Use one focal proof at compact densities and expose method, uncertainty, and controls progressively.

**Originality risk:** Low.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-03`, `RQ-04`.

**Validation test:** Measure time to identify claim, evidence class, result, source, and next action. If added detail slows the target receiver without improving accuracy, reduce or defer it.

### `AP-017` Parent or product identity collapse at small sizes

**Pattern:** Small outputs show only the parent, only the product, or an unreadable full lockup. Product territory collapses into a label, gradient dot, or cropped hero.

**Why it fails Mez:** Ads, email headers, favicons, app tiles, report footers, and shared artefacts often appear without surrounding context. Receivers must understand both family and product without four unrelated mini-brands.

**Signals:** Product name clipped; parent endorsement disappears; horizontal subnavigation overflows; colour is the only product cue; a screenshot cannot be attributed when detached.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `RQ-05`; `LIN-E01`; `STRIPE-E-003`; `ELV-E01`, `ELV-E02`, `ELV-E15`; `RAMP-E-005`, `RAMP-E-006`. Cross-source evidence is weaker than for the top three RQs.

**Exceptions:** A favicon may show a compact approved mark alone. The full relationship must be recoverable in the destination or accessible label.

**Repair strategy:** Define responsive parent, product, and endorsement levels. Add one product cue tied to job or proof territory, not only colour. Test free-floating and low-resolution contexts.

**Originality risk:** Medium. Do not copy Stripe's local rail, ElevenLabs' holding-device system, or Linear's mark hierarchy.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-02`, `RQ-05`.

**Validation test:** Test at favicon, mobile header, ad corner, email header, report footer, and detached screenshot sizes. Reviewers must correctly identify parent and product relationship.

### `AP-018` Unbounded machine variation

**Pattern:** An LLM receives broad taste guidance and is allowed to invent composition, product semantics, claims, evidence, states, and source use without a structured contract or approval gate.

**Why it fails Mez:** LLMs will be the primary system users. Unlimited freedom creates plausible but inconsistent identity, fabricated proof, copied reference signatures, and high human review burden.

**Signals:** Prompt-only brand packs; no allowed or prohibited transformations; output omits provenance; IDs are invented; the same request produces semantically different product claims; no stop gate.

**Evidence or cross-source basis:** Comparative `M-06`, `M-07`, tension 5, and E-05; `NTN-E18` to `NTN-E20`; `LIN-E12` to `LIN-E14`; `STRIPE-E-006` to `STRIPE-E-009`; `ELV-E15` to `ELV-E17`; `RAMP-E-011` to `RAMP-E-013`.

**Exceptions:** Early divergent exploration may allow broad visual variation when it uses approved content, records provenance, excludes source signatures, and cannot publish or promote itself.

**Repair strategy:** Provide stable object and pattern IDs, required fields, allowed variation, prohibited transformations, evidence rules, channel bounds, validation, and explicit human approval for high-judgement territory decisions.

**Originality risk:** Critical when reference material is present without exclusion rules.

**Related RQs:** `RQ-01`, `RQ-02`, `RQ-03`, `RQ-07`, `RQ-08`.

**Validation test:** Give the same task to two LLMs and one human. Compare semantic accuracy, evidence integrity, source-signature risk, family consistency, and review minutes. Failure in any high-risk field blocks promotion.

## Atlas-level validation

The atlas is ready for H2 preparation only when:

1. Every proposed territory is reviewed against all relevant stable IDs.
2. Exceptions are documented with purpose, evidence, owner, and expiry or review date.
3. E-01 through E-05 include an originality and anti-pattern review.
4. At least one dedicated anti-reference cycle tests whether the current list misses common failure modes outside the five-brand panel.
5. No anti-pattern is converted directly into a token, component, or visual opposite. The repair must respond to a Mez requirement and survive testing.
